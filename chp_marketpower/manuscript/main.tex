\author{Chen Ling, and~Chenye Wu,~\IEEEmembership{Senior Member,~IEEE}%
\thanks{C. Ling and C. Wu are with the School of Science and Engineering, The Chinese University of Hong Kong (Shenzhen), Shenzhen 518172, Guangdong, China, and also with Shenzhen Loop Area Institute, Shenzhen 518045, Guangdong, China.}}

\begin{abstract}
Profitable strategic offers in multiperiod electricity markets depend on how prices respond and how competing generators replace scheduled production. This paper develops continuous-offer, settlement-consistent profit evaluation under network-constrained convex hull pricing and complete reported lost-opportunity-cost compensation. A reusable selected-price map is combined with globally supported unit commitment regions, reducing a continuous offer range to finitely many endpoint and physical-schedule candidates. The resulting mechanism has two interacting forces: price responses and self-scheduling opportunities determine profit accumulation, while costly generation substitution changes physical execution and bounds the continuation of that profit path. In a modified IEEE 30-bus case, an all-day incremental offer earns 7,811.56 dollars of additional profit while uplift falls. Price-related gains accumulate before earlier operation of a competing unit becomes economic at 13.204 USD/MWh. Raising the competitor's online cost moves this threshold to 13.442 USD/MWh. Relaxing a transmission constraint instead admits a cheaper operating schedule. IEEE 30- and 118-bus pricing workloads demonstrate computational reuse. The framework links continuous strategic-profit evaluation to the operating costs and network feasibility that shape competing generation schedules.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Convex hull pricing, market power, parametric optimization, strategic offers, unit commitment, uplift payment.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{S}{trategic} offers can earn additional producer profit as prices and settlement respond, while competing generation shapes how long a given operating opportunity persists. In a multiperiod electricity market, a competitor may be able to supply the same energy only by starting earlier, remaining online longer, or transferring power through a constrained network. These choices determine how long an incumbent operating schedule remains economic as its offer rises. Convex hull pricing (CHP) incorporates unit commitment (UC) nonconvexities into price formation through convexified system cost~\cite{gribik2007,hua2017}. Understanding market power under CHP therefore requires following both the producer's settlement and the complete operating schedules that compete to serve demand.

Existing studies establish that reducing lost-opportunity costs and encouraging truthful offers are distinct market-design objectives~\cite{sun2021strategic,wang2023incentive}. A higher offer can change nodal prices, the executable UC schedule, and compensation calculated from reported costs. Additional actual profit then depends on energy revenue, uplift payment, and actual operating cost. Prices may change continuously while integer physical execution switches abruptly. Evaluating a few sampled offers can miss the favorable side of such a switch and leaves the interval between samples unexplained. A continuous assessment must locate these boundaries and connect prices to executable schedules and complete settlement.

We ask how to evaluate a producer's additional profit over a continuous offer range under multiperiod, network-constrained CHP, and how competing generation schedules shape that opportunity. The method combines a reusable CHP price map with common optimal physical schedules and complete reported lost-opportunity-cost (LOC) compensation. This construction makes the economic mechanism explicit. Price responses and the producer's best self-schedule determine how profit accumulates between physical switches. The cost of using a competing operating schedule determines when substitution becomes economic and changes the profit path. An operating-cost change can move that threshold. A network change can introduce a different competitor. Modified IEEE systems provide computational evidence and a detailed G1--G5 substitution case linking these two forces.

\subsection{Related Work}
Research on nonconvex market incentives distinguishes following an instructed dispatch from reporting actual costs. Wang \emph{et al.}~\cite{wang2023incentive} analyzed these properties under alternative pricing and compensation schemes. Byers and Eldridge~\cite{byers2022auction} studied strategic self-commitment and self-scheduling through simulated bidder behavior in multiperiod markets. CHP-specific research already provides profit-based measures of market power. Sun \emph{et al.}~\cite{sun2021strategic} developed a profit decomposition and a maximal-markup measure. Their related work~\cite{sun2020market} connects strategic gains to generator removal costs. Wu and Sun~\cite{wu2021potential} examined fixed- and variable-cost offer manipulation. We build on this settlement perspective to evaluate a continuous offer range with complete physical execution, and to explain how price-related gains interact with competing operating schedules.

The distinction between influencing prices and earning additional profit is particularly relevant across time. Sun and Wu~\cite{sun2021temporal} derived offer-to-price sensitivities and a temporal vulnerability assessment for CHP. Such sensitivities describe how an offer perturbation propagates through the price vector. The producer's settlement additionally depends on the executable schedule, its best self-schedule at those prices, and the difference between reported and actual costs. We retain this temporal price-response perspective and connect it to a profit characterization over a finite offer range. The all-day and single-hour offer directions in the numerical study expose how the admissible timing of a strategy changes its profitable opportunities.

Computing these objects requires a reliable CHP representation. Hua and Baldick~\cite{hua2017} developed the convex-primal framework based on unit feasible-set hulls and convex cost envelopes. Extended UC formulations~\cite{pan2018,yu2019} and network-flow constructions~\cite{networkflow} attach dispatch and cost to legal operating intervals. Dantzig--Wolfe decomposition provides another exact computational route through unit-level subproblems~\cite{andrianesis2022dw}. These methods supply the convexified pricing problem used here. The network-flow foundation is retained in direct power coordinates, with the same operating and boundary conventions as physical clearing and self-scheduling. This consistency matters because changing a primal UC representation can change CHP~\cite{zhao2023dependence}.

Parametric linear programming supplies the value-function and duality properties used to reuse optimization information~\cite{bertsimas1997}. In the CHP literature, Jiang and Wu~\cite{jiang2026minkowski} developed a demand-to-price mapping using optimal supply sets and an approximated Minkowski sum. The present parameter enters reported cost rather than demand. Its economic output is also different: a legal price path must be combined with integer UC regions and reported-cost settlement to evaluate actual profit. Our method assembles these three objects into a continuous-offer evaluation procedure. Common primal and complete dual information establish the selected-price intervals. Global UC bounds establish executable-schedule intervals. Their intersection supports finite profit candidates.

\subsection{Our Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Continuous-offer, settlement-consistent strategic-profit evaluation.} Affine offer changes update the objective of a fixed network-flow CHP model. Common primal optima and complete endpoint duals construct a reusable selected-price map, while feasible schedule cost lines and global UC bounds identify executable physical regions. Their intersection makes actual profit convex on each branch under complete reported LOC and reduces the continuous offer range to finite endpoint/physical-schedule candidates. The evaluation retains the distinction between a boundary maximum and an approachable one-sided profit.
\item \emph{Characterizing profitable offers through price responses and competing operating schedules.} A local profit relation separates the price response weighted by the best self-schedule from the difference between actual and self-scheduled offer exposure. Integrated over the offer path, it explains how gains accumulate before costly generation substitution changes execution. Complete operating schedules identify the replacement cost and its conditional switching threshold. Operating-cost and transmission comparisons then distinguish a change in the cost of an existing substitute from the admission of a different substitute, with their market consequences evaluated through CHP prices and full settlement.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the profit opportunity, its competing schedules, the effects of changed market conditions, and computational performance. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Market Model and Strategic Offers}
\label{sec:model}

This section establishes the physical market-clearing, CHP, and unit self-scheduling models used to evaluate strategic offers. Physical UC determines executable dispatch instructions and actual operating expenses. An interval network-flow formulation expresses CHP as a linear program (LP) whose feasible region remains invariant under offer changes. Linking physical clearing and market prices through complete LOC compensation establishes the settlement foundation for assessing realized producer profits.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Physical Market Clearing and Strategic Offers}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Consider a day-ahead electricity market over hourly trading periods $\mathcal{T}\!=\!\{1,\ldots,T\}$, with in-horizon transitions $\mathcal{T}^{+}\!=\!\{2,\ldots,T\}$. Let $\mathcal{G}$, $\mathcal{B}$, and $\mathcal{L}$ denote the sets of generators, buses, and transmission lines, respectively. Each generator $i\!\in\!\mathcal{G}$ is located at bus $b(i)\!\in\!\mathcal{B}$, and its physical schedule is $s_i\!\!=\!\!(\bp_i,\bu_i)$, where $p_{i,t}\!\!\ge\!\! 0$ denotes active power output and $u_{i,t}\!\in\!\{0,1\}$ denotes binary commitment status in period $t\!\in\!\mathcal{T}$.

Each generator is characterized by output limits $(\Pmin_i,\Pmax_i)$, normal ramp-up and ramp-down limits $(\Rup_i,\Rdn_i)$, startup and shutdown ramp capabilities $(\Sup_i,\Sdn_i)$ satisfying $\Sup_i,\Sdn_i\ge\Pmin_i$, and minimum up and down times $(\Mup_i,\Mdn_i)$. The inherited status $u_{i,0}\in\{0,1\}$, initial output $p_{i,0}\ge 0$, and preceding online or offline duration determine the initial operating conditions and residual minimum up/down-time requirements at the beginning of the horizon. Let $v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SU}},v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SD}}\in\{0,1\}$ denote binary startup and shutdown indicators in period $t\in\mathcal{T}$. Feasible commitment trajectories satisfy the inherited residual requirements and, for all $t\in\mathcal{T}$,
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:commitment}
\begin{align}
 &u_{i,t}-u_{i,t-1} = v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SU}}-v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SD}},\\
 &v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SU}}+v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SD}} \le 1,\\
 &\sum\nolimits_{\tau=\max\{1,t-\Mup_i+1\}}^{t} v_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{SU}} \le u_{i,t},\\
 &\sum\nolimits_{\tau=\max\{1,t-\Mdn_i+1\}}^{t} v_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{SD}} \le 1-u_{i,t}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

Active power trajectories satisfy the capacity and transition-dependent ramping constraints:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:physical}
\begin{align}
 \Pmin_i u_{i,t} &\le p_{i,t}\le\Pmax_i u_{i,t},\\
 p_{i,t}-p_{i,t-1} &\le \Rup_i u_{i,t-1} + \Sup_i v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SU}},\\
 p_{i,t-1}-p_{i,t} &\le \Rdn_i u_{i,t} + \Sdn_i v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SD}},
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

For all $t\in\mathcal{T}$, with the initial transition evaluated from $(u_{i,0},p_{i,0})$. No artificial shutdown is imposed at the end of the horizon. Let $\mathcal{S}_i$ denote the resulting set of physically feasible schedules $s_i=(\bp_i,\bu_i)$. The same physical operating conditions underpin UC clearing, unit self-scheduling, and the unit convex hulls used for CHP~\cite{zhao2023dependence}.

Generator $i$ incurs fixed startup cost $C_i^{\mathrm{SU}}$, shutdown cost $C_i^{\mathrm{SD}}$, hourly no-load cost $C_i^{\mathrm{NL}}$ (covering operation at minimum output $\Pmin_i$), and convex piecewise-linear (PWL) incremental fuel cost $\phi_i(q)=\max\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{K}_i}\{\sigma_{i,k}q+\gamma_{i,k}\}$ above $\Pmin_i$, with $\phi_i(0)=0$. Here $k\in\mathcal{K}_i$ indexes the affine segments with slopes $\sigma_{i,k}$ and intercepts $\gamma_{i,k}$. With in-horizon startup and shutdown counts $n_i^{\mathrm{SU}}=\sum\nolimits_{t\in\mathcal{T}}v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SU}}$ and $n_i^{\mathrm{SD}}=\sum\nolimits_{t\in\mathcal{T}}v_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SD}}$, the actual operating cost is
\begin{align}
C_i(s_i) ={}& C_i^{\mathrm{SU}}n_i^{\mathrm{SU}} + C_i^{\mathrm{SD}}n_i^{\mathrm{SD}}
+ C_i^{\mathrm{NL}}\!\sum\nolimits_{t\in\mathcal{T}}u_{i,t}\notag\\[-2pt]
&+ \sum\nolimits_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_i\!\left(p_{i,t}-\Pmin_i u_{i,t}\right).\label{eq:cost}
\end{align}

To evaluate strategic offers over a continuous range, we designate generator $g\in\mathcal{G}$ as the strategic unit whose offer varies, while competing generators $i\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\{g\}$ report their actual costs $\widehat{C}_i(s_i)=C_i(s_i)$. Generator $g$ modifies its incremental energy-offer slopes in period $t\in\mathcal{T}$ from $\sigma_{g,k}$ to $\sigma_{g,k}+\delta w_t$ for all $k\in\mathcal{K}_g$, where $\delta\in\mathcal{D}=[0,\bar\delta]$ is a scalar offer adder in USD/MWh and $\bm{w}=(w_t)_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\ge\bm{0}$ is a fixed temporal offer profile. Shifting every segment slope in period $t$ by $\delta w_t$ yields the reported operating cost
\begin{align}
\widehat{C}_g(s_g;\delta) &= C_g(s_g) + \delta Q_g(s_g),\label{eq:reported-cost}\\
Q_g(s_g) &= \sum\nolimits_{t\in\mathcal{T}} w_t\!\left(p_{g,t}-\Pmin_g u_{g,t}\right),\label{eq:exposure}
\end{align}
where $Q_g(s_g)$ (in MWh) denotes the weighted amount of generation above minimum output to which the offer adder applies (hereafter, offer-affected incremental generation). Setting $w_t=1$ for all $t\in\mathcal{T}$ represents a uniform all-day markup, whereas setting nonzero weights in selected periods represents targeted hourly offers. Physical operating limits and fixed-cost parameters remain unchanged across $\mathcal{D}$.

Let $D_{b,t}$ denote demand at bus $b\in\mathcal{B}$, $H_{l,b}$ the power transfer distribution factor (PTDF) of line $l\in\mathcal{L}$ with respect to bus $b$, and $f_l^{\max}$ the line flow limit. The system-feasible physical schedule set $\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$ requires $s_i\in\mathcal{S}_i$ for all $i\in\mathcal{G}$ together with system power balance and transmission constraints:
\begin{align}
&\sum\nolimits_{i\in\mathcal{G}} p_{i,t} = \sum\nolimits_{b\in\mathcal{B}} D_{b,t}, \ \forall t\in\mathcal{T},\label{eq:balance}\\
&f_{l,t} \!\!=\!\! \sum\nolimits_{i\in\mathcal{G}} H_{l,b(i)}p_{i,t} \!\!- \!\!\sum\nolimits_{b\!\in\!\mathcal{B}} H_{l,b}D_{b,t}, \ \forall l\!\!\in\!\!\mathcal{L},\ \forall t\!\!\in\!\!\mathcal{T},\label{eq:network}\\
&-f_l^{\max} \le f_{l,t} \le f_l^{\max}, \ \forall l\in\mathcal{L},\ \forall t\in\mathcal{T}.\label{eq:powerflow}
\end{align}

Writing $C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s)=\sum\nolimits_{i\in\mathcal{G}}C_i(s_i)$ for the actual system operating cost, the independent system operator (ISO) clears the physical market by solving the MILP:
\begin{equation}
v^{\UC}(\delta) = \min\nolimits_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{\UC}}
\left\{ C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s) + \delta Q_g(s_g) \right\}.\label{eq:physical-clearing}
\end{equation}

Solving~\eqref{eq:physical-clearing} determines the executable UC schedule $s^{\UC}(\delta)=(\bp^{\UC}(\delta),\bu^{\UC}(\delta))$, which governs actual physical dispatch and production costs.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Network-Flow CHP and Its Parametric Form}
\label{subsec:dag-model}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Whereas physical clearing~\eqref{eq:physical-clearing} selects binary-feasible unit schedules, CHP derives nodal prices from a convexified system problem~\cite{gribik2007,hua2017}. Because $Q_g(s_g)$ in~\eqref{eq:exposure} depends on both output and commitment status, we define the actual operating cost epigraph for each generator $i\in\mathcal{G}$ in the lifted power--commitment--cost space:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Y}_i \!\!=\!\! \left\{ (\bp_i,\bu_i,\eta_i) \!\!:\!\! s_i\!\!=\!\!(\bp_i,\bu_i)\!\!\in\!\!\mathcal{S}_i,\ \eta_i\!\!\ge\!\! C_i(s_i) \right\}, \ \forall i\in\mathcal{G}.\label{eq:unit-hull}
\end{equation}

Because the strategic offer adjustment $\delta Q_g(\bp_g,\bu_g)$ enters affinely, the convexified pricing problem optimizes over the fixed unit hulls $\conv(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ for all generators $i\in\mathcal{G}$ (including the strategic unit $g$):
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:hull-clearing}
\begin{align}
v^{\CHP}(\delta) = &\min_{\bp,\bu,\bm\eta,\bm f}\quad  \sum\nolimits_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\eta_i + \delta Q_g(\bp_g,\bu_g)\\
&\mathrm{s.t.}\quad  (\bp_i,\bu_i,\eta_i)\in\conv(\mathcal{Y}_i), \quad \forall i\in\mathcal{G},\\
& \eqref{eq:balance}\text{--}\eqref{eq:powerflow}.\notag
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

An interval network-flow formulation represents $\conv(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ explicitly as a polynomial-size linear system~\cite{pan2018,yu2019,networkflow}. For each generator $i\in\mathcal{G}$, a time-expanded directed acyclic graph (DAG) $\mathfrak{G}_i=(\mathcal{V}_i,\mathcal{E}_i)$ alternates between admissible online ($\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}$) and offline ($\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{OFF}}$) intervals, where $\mathcal{E}_i=\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}\cup\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{OFF}}$, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:dag-schematic}. The node set $\mathcal{V}_i$ consists of a source node representing the inherited operating state at $t=0$, a terminal sink node, and internal boundary event nodes $V_t^{\mathrm{on}}$ and $V_t^{\mathrm{off}}$ that mark transitions into ON intervals (startup at period $t$) and OFF intervals (shutdown at period $t$), respectively, for $t\in\mathcal{T}^{+}$. An ON arc $e=[\underline{t}_e,\overline{t}_e]\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}$ ($1\le\underline{t}_e\le\overline{t}_e\le T$) represents continuous online operation from period $\underline{t}_e$ through $\overline{t}_e$ of duration $n_e=\overline{t}_e-\underline{t}_e+1$; it connects $V_{\underline{t}_e}^{\mathrm{on}}$ (or the source if $\underline{t}_e=1$) to $V_{\overline{t}_e+1}^{\mathrm{off}}$, or directly to the sink if $\overline{t}_e=T$. OFF arcs $a=[\underline{t}_a,\overline{t}_a]\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{OFF}}$ are defined analogously. Admissible arcs respect the minimum up/down times, ramping limits, and inherited boundary conditions defining $\mathcal{S}_i$.

Assigning a nonnegative flow weight $z_{i,e}$ to each ON arc $e\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}$ and $z_{i,a}$ to each OFF arc $a\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{OFF}}$, routing one unit of flow from the source to the sink enforces alternating transitions:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:flow}
\begin{align}
&\sum\nolimits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}:\overline{t}_e=t-1} z_{i,e} \!=\! \sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{OFF}}:\underline{t}_a=t} z_{i,a}, \ \forall t\!\!\in\!\!\mathcal{T}^{+},\label{eq:flow-on-off}\\
&\sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{OFF}}:\overline{t}_a=t-1} z_{i,a} \!=\! \sum\nolimits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}:\underline{t}_e=t} z_{i,e}, \ \forall t\!\!\in\!\!\mathcal{T}^{+},\label{eq:flow-off-on}\\
&\sum\nolimits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}:\underline{t}_e=1} z_{i,e} + \sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{OFF}}:\underline{t}_a=1} z_{i,a} = 1.\label{eq:flow-source}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

Each source-to-sink path represents a feasible commitment trajectory, while fractional arc flows represent convex combinations of such trajectories. Different paths may share an ON interval, whose flow weight equals the total weight of the paths using it. To preserve the operating characteristics of these fractional combinations, the power, ramping limits, and cost epigraph associated with each ON arc are scaled by its flow weight.

Specifically, let $p_{i,e,t}$ and $c_{i,e,t}$ denote the perspective-scaled active power and variable-cost epigraph variables on ON arc $e$ for period $t\in e$ (i.e., $\underline{t}_e\le t\le\overline{t}_e$). Scaling the physical interval constraints by the arc-flow weight $z_{i,e}$ gives
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:interval}
\begin{align}
\Pmin_i z_{i,e} &\le p_{i,e,t}\le\Pmax_i z_{i,e}, && \underline{t}_e\le t\le\overline{t}_e,\\
p_{i,e,t}-p_{i,e,t-1} &\le \Rup_i z_{i,e}, && \underline{t}_e<t\le\overline{t}_e,\\
p_{i,e,t-1}-p_{i,e,t} &\le \Rdn_i z_{i,e}, && \underline{t}_e<t\le\overline{t}_e,\\
p_{i,e,\underline{t}_e} &\le \Sup_i z_{i,e}, && \text{startup at }\underline{t}_e,\\
p_{i,e,\overline{t}_e} &\le \Sdn_i z_{i,e}, && \overline{t}_e<T,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where an inherited online interval ($\underline{t}_e=1,u_{i,0}=1$) replaces the startup bound at $t=1$ with $-\Rdn_i z_{i,e}\le p_{i,e,1}-p_{i,0}z_{i,e}\le\Rup_i z_{i,e}$. The perspective-scaled PWL variable-cost epigraph satisfies
\begin{equation}
\!\!\!\!c_{i,e,t}\!\! \ge\!\! \sigma_{i,k}\!\left(p_{i,e,t}\!\!-\!\!\Pmin_i z_{i,e}\right) \!\!+\!\! \gamma_{i,k}z_{i,e},  \forall k\!\!\in\!\!\mathcal{K}_i,\ \underline{t}_e\!\!\le\!\! t\!\!\le\!\!\overline{t}_e.\label{eq:perspective}
\end{equation}

Let $\kappa_{i,e}$ denote the fixed cost assigned to arc $e\in\mathcal{E}_i$: each ON arc carries $n_e C_i^{\mathrm{NL}}$ plus any applicable startup and shutdown charges at its boundaries, and an immediate-shutdown OFF arc at $t=1$ carries $C_i^{\mathrm{SD}}$ (zero for all other OFF arcs). Summing the interval contributions reconstructs the convexified power, commitment status, and operating cost:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:aggregation}
\begin{align}
&p_{i,t} = \sum\nolimits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}:t\in e} p_{i,e,t}, \ \forall t\in\mathcal{T},\label{eq:agg-power}\\
&u_{i,t} = \sum\nolimits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}:t\in e} z_{i,e}, \ \forall t\in\mathcal{T},\label{eq:agg-status}\\
&\eta_i \!\!\ge\!\! \sum\nolimits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_i}\kappa_{i,e}z_{i,e}
\!\!+\!\! \sum\nolimits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_i^{\mathrm{ON}}}\sum\nolimits_{t\in e}c_{i,e,t}.\label{eq:agg-cost}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

When $z_{i,e}>0$, normalizing $(p_{i,e,t},c_{i,e,t})$ by $z_{i,e}$ recovers a feasible physical ON-interval trajectory and its PWL cost epigraph. Flow decomposition and perspective scaling jointly preserve the power--commitment--cost relationship under convex combinations, so the projection of~\eqref{eq:flow}--\eqref{eq:aggregation} onto $(\bp_i,\bu_i,\eta_i)$ equals $\conv(\mathcal{Y}_i)$~\cite{pan2018,networkflow}.

For parametric analysis, collect all unit-level network-flow variables across $i\in\mathcal{G}$ and the system constraints~\eqref{eq:balance}--\eqref{eq:powerflow} into a fixed CHP feasible polyhedron $\mathcal{P}$, with $x$ denoting the extended CHP decision vector. Let $c^0$ collect the baseline cost coefficients so that $(c^0)^\top x=\sum\nolimits_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\eta_i$, and let $d$ collect the coefficients of the strategic offer adder such that $d^\top x=Q_g(\bp_g,\bu_g)$ for the convexified quantities $(\bp_g,\bu_g)$ represented by $x$. The CHP pricing problem~\eqref{eq:hull-clearing} then takes the compact objective-parametric LP form
\begin{equation}
v^{\CHP}(\delta) = \min\nolimits_{x\in\mathcal{P}} (c^0 + \delta d)^\top x.\label{eq:paramlp}
\end{equation}
Because the offer adder modifies only the objective coefficients, $\mathcal{P}$ is independent of $\delta$.

Let $\lambda_t^E$ denote the dual multiplier associated with power balance~\eqref{eq:balance} under the Lagrangian term $\lambda_t^E(\sum_b D_{b,t}-\sum_i p_{i,t})$, and let $\mu_{l,t}^{+},\mu_{l,t}^{-}\ge 0$ denote the dual multipliers on the upper and lower transmission limits in~\eqref{eq:powerflow}. The nodal CHP price at bus $b\in\mathcal{B}$ in period $t\in\mathcal{T}$ is
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{b,t}^{\CHP} = \lambda_t^E + \lambda_{b,t}^C, \
\lambda_{b,t}^C = -\sum\nolimits_{l\in\mathcal{L}} H_{l,b}\!\left(\mu_{l,t}^{+}-\mu_{l,t}^{-}\right).\label{eq:lmp}
\end{equation}
While the energy component $\lambda_t^E$ and congestion component $\lambda_{b,t}^C$ depend on the chosen PTDF reference bus, the nodal prices $\lambda_{b,t}^{\CHP}$ and resulting settlements are reference-invariant.


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Self-Scheduling, Uplift, and Actual Profit}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Market settlement links three distinct optimization objects: the executable physical UC schedule $s^{\UC}(\delta)$ from~\eqref{eq:physical-clearing}, the convexified CHP pricing solution $x^{\CHP}(\delta)$ from~\eqref{eq:paramlp} whose dual multipliers define $\blambda^{\CHP}(\delta)$, and the strategic generator's best self-schedule $s_g^{\SSched}(\delta)$. Facing its bus price vector $\blambda_g(\delta)=(\lambda_{b(g),t}^{\CHP}(\delta))_{t\in\mathcal{T}}$, generator $g$ maximizes its reported self-scheduling profit over $\mathcal{S}_g$:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Pi}_g^{\SSched}(\blambda_g,\delta)
= \max\nolimits_{s_g\in\mathcal{S}_g}
\left\{ \blambda_g^\top\bp_g - \widehat{C}_g(s_g;\delta) \right\}.\label{eq:ss}
\end{equation}

Because unit operating profit is additive across ON and OFF intervals,~\eqref{eq:ss} is solved on the same DAG $\mathfrak{G}_g$ by evaluating the unscaled interval LP ($z_{g,e}=1$) on each ON arc and extracting a maximum-weight source-to-sink path via dynamic programming.

Under the physical schedule $s_g^{\UC}(\delta)$ and selected price vector $\blambda_g(\delta)$, generator $g$ receives energy-market revenue $R_g(\delta)$ and complete reported lost-opportunity-cost (LOC) uplift $U_g(\delta)$. Its actual net operating profit is
\begin{align}
R_g(\delta) &= \blambda_g(\delta)^\top\bp_g^{\UC}(\delta),\label{eq:revenue}\\
U_g(\delta) &= \widehat{\Pi}_g^{\SSched}(\blambda_g(\delta),\delta)
- \left[ R_g(\delta) - \widehat{C}_g(s_g^{\UC}(\delta);\delta) \right],\label{eq:uplift}\\
\Pi_g(\delta) &= R_g(\delta) + U_g(\delta) - C_g(s_g^{\UC}(\delta)).\label{eq:profit}
\end{align}
Since $s_g^{\UC}(\delta)\in\mathcal{S}_g$ is feasible for~\eqref{eq:ss}, $U_g(\delta)\ge 0$. Reported costs $\widehat{C}_g$ determine LOC compensation, whereas actual operating costs $C_g$ determine realized producer profit. Substituting~\eqref{eq:reported-cost} and~\eqref{eq:uplift} into~\eqref{eq:profit} yields the settlement identity
\begin{equation}
\Pi_g(\delta) = \widehat{\Pi}_g^{\SSched}(\blambda_g(\delta),\delta)
+ \delta Q_g(s_g^{\UC}(\delta)).\label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
This identity expresses actual profit as the reported self-scheduling profit plus the markup-related settlement term associated with the executed UC schedule.

We evaluate the strategic generator's additional actual profit over the continuous offer range $\mathcal{D}=[0,\bar\delta]$, holding all other generators' offers fixed:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_g = \max\nolimits_{\delta\in\mathcal{D}}\Delta\Pi_g(\delta), \quad
\Delta\Pi_g(\delta) = \Pi_g(\delta) - \Pi_g(0),\label{eq:marketpower}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_g(\delta)$ is evaluated under a specified CHP price selection and physical tie-breaking rule formalized in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

For a specified CHP price selection $\blambda_g(\delta)$ and physical execution selection $s^{\UC}(\delta)\in\mathcal{S}^{\UC,*}(\delta)$, the generator's additional actual profit relative to truthful bidding is
% \begin{equation}
% \Delta\Pi_g(\delta;s^{\UC}(\delta)) = \Pi_g(\delta;s^{\UC}(\delta)) - \Pi_g(0).\label{eq:marketpower}

Because the network-flow formulation~\eqref{eq:paramlp} provides a fixed CHP feasible polyhedron $\mathcal{P}$ and the physical clearing problem~\eqref{eq:physical-clearing} operates over a fixed feasible set $\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$, we next exploit this parametric structure to reuse pricing and scheduling information across $\mathcal{D}$ rather than solving each offer instance independently.

% SECTION III: PARAMETRIC CHP ORACLE FOR STRATEGIC PROFIT EVALUATION
% =====================================================================
\section{Parametric CHP Oracle for Strategic Profit Evaluation}
\label{sec:method}

Evaluating strategic offers over a continuous range typically requires repeatedly re-solving UC, pricing, and self-scheduling models as the offer changes. Because an offer adder perturbs only objective coefficients against fixed constraint polyhedra, these repeated optimizations can be compiled into reusable parametric structures. We establish an offline affine price oracle, identify intervals over which complete physical schedules remain optimal, and prove that actual producer profit is convex within each joint region. This structure reduces continuous profit evaluation across an entire offer domain to an exact search over finitely many boundary candidates.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Parametric CHP Price Response}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
For any fixed convexified solution $x\in\mathcal{P}$ in~\eqref{eq:paramlp}, its reported system cost is affine in the offer adder $\delta\in\mathcal{D}$:
\begin{equation}
\ell_x^P(\delta) = (c^0)^\top x + \delta d^\top x.\label{eq:primal-line}
\end{equation}
The intercept $(c^0)^\top x$ represents its baseline convexified cost, while $d^\top x=Q_g(\bp_g,\bu_g)$ is the convexified offer-affected incremental generation. Because~\eqref{eq:paramlp} minimizes these cost functions over all $x\in\mathcal{P}$, the CHP value function $v^{\CHP}(\delta)=\min\nolimits_{x\in\mathcal{P}}\ell_x^P(\delta)$ is concave and piecewise affine in $\delta$~\cite{bertsimas1997}. Each affine segment corresponds to an offer interval over which a common convexified solution $\bar{x}\in\mathcal{P}$ remains optimal.

The cost envelope characterizes the response of the CHP optimal value, whose slope on each segment equals $d^\top\bar{x}$. Market settlement, however, depends on nodal prices obtained from the optimal dual multipliers. We therefore construct an affine dual-price selection over each certified interval. Let $\mathcal{F}^D(\delta)$ denote the feasible set of the complete dual of~\eqref{eq:paramlp}, including variable-bound multipliers, and let $L(y)$ denote its dual objective. Because $\delta$ enters only the primal objective, dual feasibility depends affinely on $\delta$, whereas $L(y)$ is independent of $\delta$. A common primal optimizer establishes the persistence of the optimal cost function across an interval, while dual interpolation establishes a valid affine selection of the corresponding CHP prices.

\begin{proposition}[Affine Selected CHP Prices]
\label{prop:dual}
Suppose a common primal solution $\bar{x}\in\mathcal{P}$ is optimal for~\eqref{eq:paramlp} at $\delta^-<\delta^+$, with complete optimal dual solutions $y^-\in\mathcal{F}^D(\delta^-)$ and $y^+\in\mathcal{F}^D(\delta^+)$ at the respective endpoints. For any $\delta\in[\delta^-,\delta^+]$, define $\theta=(\delta-\delta^-)/(\delta^+-\delta^-)\in[0,1]$. Then the interpolated dual solution
\begin{equation}
y(\delta) = (1-\theta)y^- + \theta y^+\label{eq:dual-interpolation}
\end{equation}
is optimal throughout $[\delta^-,\delta^+]$. Consequently, the corresponding nodal CHP prices define an affine selected-price function:
\begin{equation}
\blambda^{\CHP}(\delta) = (1-\theta)\blambda^- + \theta\blambda^+ = \bm{\alpha} + \delta\bm{\beta},\label{eq:interval-affine-price}
\end{equation}
where $\blambda^-$ and $\blambda^+$ are the nodal-price projections~\eqref{eq:lmp} of $y^-$ and $y^+$, with $\bm{\alpha}=\blambda^--\delta^-\bm{\beta}$ and
\begin{equation}
\bm{\beta} = \frac{\blambda^+ - \blambda^-}{\delta^+ - \delta^-} = \frac{d\blambda^{\CHP}}{d\delta}.\label{eq:price-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The convex combination~\eqref{eq:dual-interpolation} preserves dual feasibility because the constraints in $\mathcal{F}^D(\delta)$ depend affinely on $\delta$. By linearity of $L(\cdot)$ and strong duality at both endpoints,
\begin{align*}
L(y(\delta)) &= (1-\theta)L(y^-) + \theta L(y^+)\\
&= (1-\theta)\ell_{\bar{x}}^P(\delta^-) + \theta\ell_{\bar{x}}^P(\delta^+) = \ell_{\bar{x}}^P(\delta).
\end{align*}
Since $\bar{x}$ is primal feasible and the interpolated dual attains the same objective, both are optimal at $\delta$. The nodal-price expression~\eqref{eq:lmp} is linear in the relevant dual multipliers; substituting $\theta=(\delta-\delta^-)/(\delta^+-\delta^-)$ yields~\eqref{eq:interval-affine-price}--\eqref{eq:price-sensitivity}.
\end{proof}

In~\eqref{eq:price-sensitivity}, $\bm{\beta}$ is the offer-to-price sensitivity along the selected CHP price path (representing the derivative $d\blambda^{\CHP}/d\delta$ on the interior of $[\delta^-,\delta^+]$ and the corresponding one-sided slope at the endpoints). Unlike the primal cost slope $d^\top\bar{x}$, which measures convexified generation quantity, $\bm{\beta}$ measures how nodal prices respond to the offer adder even while the same convexified solution $\bar{x}$ remains optimal. The affine price characterization identifies what can be reused over an offer interval; we next discover these intervals and store their price functions for subsequent queries.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Offline Construction of the Price Oracle}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
We partition $\mathcal{D}$ into intervals satisfying Proposition~\ref{prop:dual} by solving~\eqref{eq:paramlp} at selected offers and testing saved primal solutions for common endpoint optimality. On any unresolved bracket $[\delta^-,\delta^+]$ with endpoint primal--dual pairs $(x^-,y^-)$ and $(x^+,y^+)$, the endpoint cost functions~\eqref{eq:primal-line} provide an upper bound on $v^{\CHP}(\delta)$, while dual interpolation provides a lower bound:
\begin{align}
U^P(\delta) &= \min\{\ell_{x^-}^P(\delta),\ell_{x^+}^P(\delta)\},\label{eq:pricing-upper}\\
L^P(\delta) &= \frac{\delta^+-\delta}{\delta^+-\delta^-}L(y^-) + \frac{\delta-\delta^-}{\delta^+-\delta^-}L(y^+).\label{eq:pricing-lower}
\end{align}
These bounds satisfy $L^P(\delta)\le v^{\CHP}(\delta)\le U^P(\delta)$ for all $\delta\in[\delta^-,\delta^+]$ and guide the construction of the price oracle. The primal cost functions evaluate previously obtained convexified schedules at a new offer, while dual interpolation bounds the optimal reported system cost from below. If a saved primal solution $\bar{x}$ is optimal at both endpoints (i.e., $\ell_{\bar{x}}^P(\delta^-)=v^{\CHP}(\delta^-)$ and $\ell_{\bar{x}}^P(\delta^+)=v^{\CHP}(\delta^+)$), Proposition~\ref{prop:dual} certifies the entire interval and supplies its affine price function.

Otherwise, the intersection of the endpoint cost functions $\ell_{x^-}^P(\delta)=\ell_{x^+}^P(\delta)$ identifies an informative offer for refinement: the two schedules have different offer-affected generation and their relative reported costs change at that point. Solving~\eqref{eq:paramlp} at this intersection (or at the midpoint if their slopes coincide) supplies a new primal--dual pair and determines whether another optimality region lies between them. Sharing the solved dual vector across the resulting sub-brackets guarantees that the selected price path remains continuous across every breakpoint.

Repeating this refinement until $\mathcal{D}$ is covered and merging adjacent segments with identical affine price coefficients $(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta})$ yields the offline CHP price Oracle:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{O}_g^{\CHP} = \left\{ \left(I_m^P,\bm{\alpha}^m,\bm{\beta}^m\right) \right\}_{m=1}^{M_P}, \qquad I_m^P = [\underline{\delta}_m^P,\overline{\delta}_m^P],\label{eq:price-oracle}
\end{equation}
which evaluates nodal prices on each interval $I_m^P$ (certified piecewise by the underlying common-primal subintervals) as
\begin{equation}
\blambda^{\CHP}(\delta) = \bm{\alpha}^m + \delta\bm{\beta}^m, \qquad \forall\delta\in I_m^P.\label{eq:affine-price}
\end{equation}
For a given market instance, strategic generator $g$, and offer profile $\bm{w}$, online evaluation at any query $\delta\in\mathcal{D}$ requires only $O(\log M_P)$ comparisons to locate $I_m^P$ followed by direct evaluation of~\eqref{eq:affine-price}. While $\mathcal{O}_g^{\CHP}$ captures the price response, actual settlement also depends on the executable UC schedule, whose response is characterized next.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Physical UC Regions}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

CHP pricing solutions and executable physical schedules can change at different offers; consequently, a price-response curve alone cannot determine realized profit. For any complete system-feasible schedule $s\in\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$, its reported system cost is an affine function of $\delta$:
\begin{equation}
\ell_s^{\UC}(\delta) = C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s) + \delta Q_g(s_g),\label{eq:uc-line}
\end{equation}
where the intercept $C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s)$ is the actual system operating cost of executing schedule $s$ and the slope $Q_g(s_g)$ is the strategic generator's offer-affected generation under $s$. As the offer adder increases, the ISO may favor a schedule with less offer-affected generation, even if that schedule requires higher operating expenses from competing units. Because $\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$ is independent of $\delta$ and comprises finitely many binary commitment states with polyhedral dispatch and PWL cost constraints, $v^{\UC}(\delta)=\min\nolimits_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{\UC}}\ell_s^{\UC}(\delta)$ is the lower envelope of finitely many affine lines and is therefore concave and piecewise affine on $\mathcal{D}$.

\begin{lemma}[Common Optimal Physical Schedule]
\label{lem:uc}
If a complete system-feasible schedule $\bar{s}\in\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$ is globally optimal for~\eqref{eq:physical-clearing} at both $\delta^-$ and $\delta^+$, then $\bar{s}$ remains globally optimal for every $\delta\in[\delta^-,\delta^+]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The affine function $\ell_{\bar{s}}^{\UC}(\delta)$ upper-bounds $v^{\UC}(\delta)$ on $[\delta^-,\delta^+]$, while concavity lower-bounds $v^{\UC}(\delta)$ by the chord connecting its endpoint values. Optimality of $\bar{s}$ at both endpoints makes that chord coincide with $\ell_{\bar{s}}^{\UC}(\delta)$ across $[\delta^-,\delta^+]$.
\end{proof}

Starting from globally optimal UC schedules at the endpoints of $\mathcal{D}$, candidate intervals $[\delta^-,\delta^+]$ are checked for a common optimal schedule. If distinct endpoint optimal schedules $s^-$ and $s^+$ have equal offer-affected generation $Q_g(s_g^-)=Q_g(s_g^+)$, their cost lines coincide and $s^-$ remains optimal across $[\delta^-,\delta^+]$; when $Q_g(s_g^-)>Q_g(s_g^+)$, the intersection $\ell_{s^-}^{\UC}(\delta)=\ell_{s^+}^{\UC}(\delta)$ identifies a candidate offer for an additional global UC solve. Recursive refinement partitions $\mathcal{D}$ into physical persistence intervals $\{I_r^{\UC}\}_{r=1}^{M_{\UC}}$, each supported by a common optimal complete schedule. Price intervals and physical UC regions capture two distinct responses to the same offer change; their intersection provides the structure needed to evaluate actual profit under complete LOC settlement.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Finite Strategic-Profit Evaluation}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Intersecting the selected-price intervals $\{I_m^P\}_{m=1}^{M_P}$ with the physical UC regions $\{I_r^{\UC}\}_{r=1}^{M_{\UC}}$ produces a covering family of joint intervals $I_j=[\underline{\delta}_j,\overline{\delta}_j]$ ($j=1,\ldots,J$). On each joint interval $I_j$, the selected CHP price is affine and a complete physical schedule $\bar{s}^{\,j}$ remains optimal throughout the interval:
\begin{align}
\blambda_g(\delta) &= \bm{\alpha}_g^j + \delta\bm{\beta}_g^j,\notag\\
\bar{s}^{\,j} &\in \arg\min\nolimits_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{\UC}}\{C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s) + \delta Q_g(s_g)\}, \quad \forall\delta\in I_j.\label{eq:joint-region}
\end{align}
Within $I_j$, the generator's optimal self-schedule $s_g^{\SSched}(\delta)$ in~\eqref{eq:ss} remains free to adjust as $\blambda_g(\delta)$ and $\delta$ vary. Let $\Pi_g^j(\delta)=\Pi_g(\delta;\bar{s}^{\,j})$ denote the actual profit branch on $I_j$ associated with executing $\bar{s}^{\,j}$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite Strategic-Profit Evaluation]
\label{prop:finite}
On any joint interval $I_j=[\underline{\delta}_j,\overline{\delta}_j]$ satisfying~\eqref{eq:joint-region}, the actual profit branch $\Pi_g^j(\delta)$ under complete reported LOC settlement is convex in $\delta$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\max\nolimits_{\delta\in[\underline{\delta}_j,\overline{\delta}_j]} \Pi_g^j(\delta)
= \max\left\{ \Pi_g^j(\underline{\delta}_j),\ \Pi_g^j(\overline{\delta}_j) \right\}.\label{eq:endpoint}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substituting~\eqref{eq:ss},~\eqref{eq:reported-cost}, and~\eqref{eq:joint-region} into the settlement identity~\eqref{eq:identity} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_g^j(\delta) ={}& \max_{s_g\in\mathcal{S}_g}\Bigl\{
(\bm{\alpha}_g^j + \delta\bm{\beta}_g^j)^\top\bp_g - C_g(s_g) - \delta Q_g(s_g)
\Bigr\}\\[-2pt]
&+ \delta Q_g(\bar{s}_g^{\,j}), \qquad \forall\delta\in I_j.
\end{aligned}\label{eq:convex-profit}
\end{equation}
For each fixed $s_g\in\mathcal{S}_g$, the bracketed expression is affine in $\delta$. Its pointwise maximum over $\mathcal{S}_g$ is convex on $I_j$, and adding the affine term $\delta Q_g(\bar{s}_g^{\,j})$ preserves convexity. A convex function on a closed interval attains its maximum at an endpoint.
\end{proof}

Although the same interval DAG $\mathfrak{G}_g$ evaluates self-scheduling at any offer, Proposition~\ref{prop:finite} shows that maximizing actual profit on $I_j$ requires solving~\eqref{eq:ss} only at the endpoints $\underline{\delta}_j$ and $\overline{\delta}_j$, without constructing internal self-scheduling breakpoints. Across all joint intervals $\{I_j\}_{j=1}^{J}$, continuous profit evaluation over $\mathcal{D}$ reduces to the finite candidate set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C} = \bigcup\nolimits_{j=1}^{J}
\left\{ (\underline{\delta}_j,\bar{s}^{\,j}),\ (\overline{\delta}_j,\bar{s}^{\,j}) \right\},\label{eq:candidate-set}
\end{equation}
with maximum candidate additional profit
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_g^{\mathrm{cand}} = \max\nolimits_{(\delta,s)\in\mathcal{C}}
\left\{ \Pi_g(\delta;s) - \Pi_g(0) \right\}.\label{eq:candidate-value}
\end{equation}
At a physical switching offer, adjacent optimal UC schedules have equal reported system costs but may yield different actual producer profits due to their distinct offer-affected generation. We therefore retain both adjacent schedules as distinct candidates in $\mathcal{C}$ at the same offer. Under the upper adjacent-plan rule, the more profitable adjacent schedule is selected at the switching offer, and the maximum in~\eqref{eq:marketpower} equals $\mathcal{M}_g^{\mathrm{cand}}$. Under other boundary tie-breaking rules, the same candidate value gives the supremum over the offer domain, which can be approached arbitrarily closely from the corresponding interval interior.

% --- 替换 Section III-D 末尾的 Algorithm 1 及其后收尾段 ---
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Parametric CHP Oracle and Profit Evaluation}
\label{alg:workflow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Market model, strategic unit $g$, offer profile $\bm{w}$, and domain $\mathcal{D}$
\State Solve CHP and UC at the endpoints of $\mathcal{D}$; initialize candidate intervals.
\Statex \textbf{Stage 1: CHP Price Oracle}
\While{an uncertified CHP interval $I$ remains}
 \If{a saved primal solution is optimal at both endpoints}
  \State Certify $I$ and store its affine dual-price function \Comment{Prop.~\ref{prop:dual}}
 \Else
  \State Solve CHP at the endpoint cost-function intersection; subdivide $I$
 \EndIf
\EndWhile
\State Assemble the price oracle $\mathcal{O}_g^{\CHP}$.
\Statex \textbf{Stage 2: Physical UC Regions}
\While{an uncertified UC interval $I$ remains}
 \If{a complete schedule is globally optimal at both endpoints}
  \State Certify $I$ and retain the schedule \Comment{Lemma~\ref{lem:uc}}
 \Else
  \State Solve UC at the competing cost-function intersection; subdivide $I$
 \EndIf
\EndWhile
\Statex \textbf{Stage 3: Profit Evaluation}
\State Intersect price and UC intervals; form endpoint--schedule candidates $\mathcal{C}$
\For{each $(\delta,s)\in\mathcal{C}$}
 \State Query $\blambda_g(\delta)$, solve self-scheduling, and evaluate $\Pi_g(\delta;s)$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathcal{O}_g^{\CHP}$ and maximizing offer--schedule candidates
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow} summarizes the three-stage evaluation procedure. Whenever an endpoint cost-function intersection does not yield a strictly interior offer, the interval midpoint serves as the fallback probe. The resulting joint regions identify both the selected price response $\bm{\beta}_g^j$ and the complete operating schedules $\bar{s}^{\,j}$ that remain optimal as the offer changes. Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} uses these objects to explain how profit accumulates within a region and how competing generation changes that profit at physical switching boundaries.

% SECTION IV: PROFIT ACCUMULATION AND GENERATION SUBSTITUTION
% =====================================================================
\section{Profit Accumulation and Generation Substitution}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Section~\ref{sec:method} provides an affine selected CHP price response and a globally optimal physical schedule on each joint offer interval. These two objects allow us to examine how strategic profit accumulates while a physical schedule remains optimal, what cost competing generation must incur to replace the strategic unit, and how changes in operating costs and transmission limits reshape that competition.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Profit Accumulation Along the Offer Path}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

Consider a joint interval $I_j=[\underline{\delta}_j,\overline{\delta}_j]$ satisfying~\eqref{eq:joint-region}, on which the selected CHP price is $\blambda_g(\delta)=\bm{\alpha}_g^j+\delta\bm{\beta}_g^j$ and a complete physical schedule $\bar{s}^{\,j}$ remains globally optimal. Let $Q_g^{\UC,j}=Q_g(\bar{s}_g^{\,j})$ denote the strategic unit's physically cleared offer-affected generation on $I_j$, and let $Q_g^{\SSched}(\delta)=Q_g(s_g^{\SSched}(\delta))$ denote the offer-affected generation under its optimal self-schedule $s_g^{\SSched}(\delta)$ in~\eqref{eq:ss}. Applying the envelope theorem to the convex profit branch~\eqref{eq:convex-profit} derived from the settlement identity~\eqref{eq:identity} yields the marginal profit response at any offer in $I_j$ where $s_g^{\SSched}(\delta)$ is unique:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Pi_g^j}{d\delta}
=
\underbrace{(\bp_g^{\SSched}(\delta))^\top\bm{\beta}_g^j}_{\text{price-response effect}}
+
\underbrace{Q_g^{\UC,j} - Q_g^{\SSched}(\delta)}_{\text{offer-affected generation difference}},
\label{eq:derivative}
\end{equation}
while at isolated offers where $s_g^{\SSched}(\delta)$ switches, the left- and right-hand derivatives take the same form evaluated at the corresponding optimal self-schedules.

Equation~\eqref{eq:derivative} identifies two distinct channels through which an offer increase translates into actual profit within a physical persistence region. First, the price-response effect $(\bp_g^{\SSched})^\top\bm{\beta}_g^j$ measures the valuation gain from the CHP price sensitivity $\bm{\beta}_g^j$. Because complete reported LOC uplift~\eqref{eq:uplift} compensates the generator up to its best self-scheduling opportunity, the price sensitivity is weighted by the self-scheduled output $\bp_g^{\SSched}(\delta)$ rather than the physically instructed output $\bp_g^{\UC,j}$. Second, the generation difference $Q_g^{\UC,j}-Q_g^{\SSched}(\delta)$ compares the offer-affected generation cleared by the ISO against that chosen in self-scheduling. Consequently, price leverage alone ($\bm{\beta}_g^j\neq\bm{0}$) does not guarantee profitable market power: whether a higher offer increases realized profit depends jointly on how nodal prices respond, how much self-scheduled output captures those price changes, and how physical clearing compares with self-scheduling.

Actual profit also changes discretely when the ISO switches between globally optimal physical schedules. Let $\delta^{\mathrm{sw}}$ denote a physical switching offer between adjacent joint intervals, with superscripts $-$ and $+$ denoting the incoming (lower-offer) and outgoing (higher-offer) branches. Because the selected CHP price path $\blambda_g(\delta)$ is continuous across $\mathcal{D}$, the optimal self-scheduling value $\widehat{\Pi}_g^{\SSched}(\blambda_g(\delta),\delta)$ is continuous at $\delta^{\mathrm{sw}}$. By the settlement identity~\eqref{eq:identity}, the resulting profit change across the switch is determined entirely by the change in physically cleared offer-affected generation:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J_g(\delta^{\mathrm{sw}})
&= \Pi_g^+(\delta^{\mathrm{sw}}) - \Pi_g^-(\delta^{\mathrm{sw}})\\
&= \delta^{\mathrm{sw}}\!\left(Q_g^{\UC,+} - Q_g^{\UC,-}\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:physical-jump}
\end{equation}

Combining continuous accumulation across joint intervals with discrete jumps at physical switches decomposes the net profit change from the truthful baseline $\delta=0$ to any target offer $\delta_f\in\mathcal{D}$:
\begin{align}
\Delta\Pi_g(\delta_f)
={}&\int_0^{\delta_f}\!
\left[
(\bp_g^{\SSched}(\delta))^\top\bm{\beta}_g(\delta)
+ Q_g^{\UC}(\delta) - Q_g^{\SSched}(\delta)
\right]d\delta\notag\\
&+ \sum_{\substack{\delta^{\mathrm{sw}}\in(0,\delta_f]\\\text{physical switches}}}
J_g(\delta^{\mathrm{sw}}),
\label{eq:path-ledger}
\end{align}
where at an endpoint switch ($\delta_f=\delta^{\mathrm{sw}}$), the jump $J_g(\delta_f)$ is included if the higher-offer physical schedule is executed and omitted under the lower-offer schedule.

Equation~\eqref{eq:path-ledger} characterizes the economic mechanisms generating profit along the offer path, complementing the ex-post settlement accounting decomposition from~\eqref{eq:profit}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Pi_g(\delta_f) = \Delta R_g(\delta_f) + \Delta U_g(\delta_f) - \Delta C_g(\delta_f).\label{eq:settlement-ledger}
\end{equation}
While~\eqref{eq:path-ledger} and~\eqref{eq:settlement-ledger} sum to the same total profit change $\Delta\Pi_g(\delta_f)$, their individual terms have distinct meanings:~\eqref{eq:settlement-ledger} records realized changes in energy-market revenue, LOC uplift, and actual production cost, whereas the price-response integral in~\eqref{eq:path-ledger} weights price changes by self-scheduled output $\bp_g^{\SSched}$ and therefore differs from the energy-revenue change $\Delta R_g$.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Costly Generation Substitution}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

Whether a profitable physical schedule persists as $\delta$ rises depends on the cost of feasible alternatives available to the ISO. In a multi-period network-constrained market, replacing strategic generator $g$'s output cannot be reduced to a single-hour merit-order swap: competing generators $i\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\{g\}$ may need to start earlier, remain online across minimum up-time windows, and respect inter-temporal ramping and PTDF transmission limits. Physical competition therefore takes place between complete system-feasible schedules in $\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$.

Consider two complete schedules $A,B\in\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$ with actual system operating costs $C_A=C_{\mathrm{sys}}(A)$, $C_B=C_{\mathrm{sys}}(B)$ and offer-affected incremental generation $Q_A=Q_g(A_g)$, $Q_B=Q_g(B_g)$ for strategic unit $g$. Suppose $C_B>C_A$ and $Q_B<Q_A$: schedule $B$ incurs a higher actual system operating cost at truthful offers (for instance, by committing a competing unit for additional hours), but reduces the generation volume exposed to unit $g$'s offer adder. From~\eqref{eq:uc-line}, the difference between their reported system costs is
\begin{equation}
\ell_B^{\UC}(\delta) - \ell_A^{\UC}(\delta)
= (C_B - C_A) - \delta(Q_A - Q_B).\label{eq:substitution-comparison}
\end{equation}
Schedule $B$ becomes lower in reported system cost than schedule $A$ once $\delta$ exceeds the pairwise substitution threshold
\begin{equation}
\delta_{AB}^{\UC} = \frac{C_B - C_A}{Q_A - Q_B}.\label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:threshold} has a direct economic interpretation: the numerator $C_B-C_A>0$ is the additional actual system operating cost required to execute the competing multi-period schedule $B$, while the denominator $Q_A-Q_B>0$ is the resulting reduction in the strategic unit's offer-affected generation. Their ratio is the offer markup (in USD/MWh) that the strategic generator can sustain before the ISO finds it economic to pay the extra real operating cost of schedule $B$ to curtail reliance on unit $g$. When $A$ and $B$ support adjacent globally optimal UC regions on the lower envelope $v^{\UC}(\delta)$, $\delta_{AB}^{\UC}$ is the exact physical switching threshold between them.

The UC minimization structure directly determines the direction of physical substitution and its impact on producer profit.

\begin{corollary}[Profit Effect of Physical Substitution]
\label{cor:exposure}
For fixed system feasibility $\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$ and fixed competing offers, globally optimal physical schedules at offers $\delta_1<\delta_2$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
Q_g^{\UC}(\delta_2) \le Q_g^{\UC}(\delta_1).\label{eq:exposure-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
Moreover, at a switching offer $\delta^{\mathrm{sw}}=\delta_{AB}^{\UC}\ge 0$ between adjacent globally optimal schedules $A$ and $B$ with $Q_B<Q_A$ and continuous selected CHP prices, the profit jump~\eqref{eq:physical-jump} satisfies
\begin{equation}
J_g(\delta^{\mathrm{sw}})
= \delta^{\mathrm{sw}}(Q_B - Q_A)
= -(C_B - C_A) \le 0.\label{eq:jump-replacement-cost}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $s_1,s_2\in\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$ be globally optimal at $\delta_1<\delta_2$. Optimality implies $C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s_1)+\delta_1 Q_g(s_{1,g})\le C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s_2)+\delta_1 Q_g(s_{2,g})$ and $C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s_2)+\delta_2 Q_g(s_{2,g})\le C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s_1)+\delta_2 Q_g(s_{1,g})$. Summing the two inequalities yields $(\delta_2-\delta_1)[Q_g(s_{2,g})-Q_g(s_{1,g})]\le 0$, which proves~\eqref{eq:exposure-monotonicity}. At $\delta^{\mathrm{sw}}=\delta_{AB}^{\UC}$, equality of reported system costs gives $\delta^{\mathrm{sw}}(Q_A-Q_B)=C_B-C_A$; substituting this relation into~\eqref{eq:physical-jump} yields~\eqref{eq:jump-replacement-cost}.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:exposure} links multi-period physical substitution costs directly to strategic profit. Within a physical persistence region, a favorable price response $(\bp_g^{\SSched})^\top\bm{\beta}_g^j$ allows profit to accumulate as $\delta$ increases; once $\delta$ crosses a physical switching threshold $\delta_{AB}^{\UC}$, however, the ISO transitions to a schedule with lower $Q_g^{\UC}$, imposing an immediate downward profit step equal in magnitude to the real system substitution cost $C_B-C_A$. Consequently, when substantial price-driven gains accumulate prior to substitution, the pre-switch boundary candidate $(\delta_{AB}^{\UC},A)$ often represents a local profit peak. Nevertheless, the physical switching threshold $\delta_{AB}^{\UC}$ and the profit-maximizing offer $\delta^{\Pi}\in\arg\max\nolimits_{\delta\in\mathcal{D}}\Delta\Pi_g(\delta)$ are distinct concepts: $\delta_{AB}^{\UC}$ is governed by reported system-cost indifference in~\eqref{eq:physical-clearing}, whereas $\delta^{\Pi}$ is determined by comparing all finite settlement candidates in $\mathcal{C}$ under Proposition~\ref{prop:finite}.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Operating Costs and Network-Dependent Competition}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

Because a physical substitution threshold~\eqref{eq:threshold} is valid conditionally on $A$ and $B$ remaining adjacent global UC optima, changes in market parameters can alter competition in two distinct ways: by shifting an active threshold within the same competition regime, or by introducing a new competing schedule that replaces the regime itself.

First, consider a perturbation $\epsilon$ to competing operating costs that modifies the actual system cost of any fixed schedule $s\in\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$ affinely as $C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s;\epsilon)=C_{\mathrm{sys}}(s;0)+\epsilon\chi_s$, with competing units' reported costs updated consistently. For schedules $A$ and $B$, the pairwise substitution threshold~\eqref{eq:threshold} shifts linearly with $\epsilon$:
\begin{equation}
\delta_{AB}^{\UC}(\epsilon)
= \delta_{AB}^{\UC}(0) + \epsilon\frac{\chi_B - \chi_A}{Q_A - Q_B}.\label{eq:threshold-shift}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\epsilon$ represents an increase in a competing generator's hourly online cost, then $\chi_s$ equals that unit's total online hours under schedule $s$. When schedule $B$ requires the competing unit to remain online for $\chi_B-\chi_A>0$ additional hours to replace unit $g$, raising $\epsilon$ increases the real cost of substitution and shifts $\delta_{AB}^{\UC}(\epsilon)$ upward at the predictable rate $(\chi_B-\chi_A)/(Q_A-Q_B)$ across the parameter range where $A$ and $B$ remain adjacent global optima.

Second, modifying transmission limits $f_l^{\max}$ in~\eqref{eq:powerflow} changes the physical feasible set $\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$. Relaxing a binding transmission constraint may admit another complete schedule $C\in\mathcal{F}^{\UC}$ whose reported system cost satisfies
\begin{equation}
\ell_C^{\UC}(\delta,\epsilon)
< \min\left\{ \ell_A^{\UC}(\delta,\epsilon),\ \ell_B^{\UC}(\delta,\epsilon) \right\}.\label{eq:third-schedule}
\end{equation}
Wherever~\eqref{eq:third-schedule} holds, schedule $C$ strictly dominates both $A$ and $B$ in~\eqref{eq:physical-clearing}, excluding the pair $(A,B)$ from the global UC lower envelope so that $\delta_{AB}^{\UC}(\epsilon)$ no longer governs physical market clearing. Because the pairwise cost differences $\ell_C^{\UC}(\delta,\epsilon)-\ell_A^{\UC}(\delta,\epsilon)$ and $\ell_C^{\UC}(\delta,\epsilon)-\ell_B^{\UC}(\delta,\epsilon)$ are affine in $(\delta,\epsilon)$ for fixed schedules $A,B,C$, strict dominance of $C$ over $(A,B)$ across any compact rectangular domain $\mathcal{R}=[\underline{\delta},\overline{\delta}]\times[\underline{\epsilon},\overline{\epsilon}]$ can be certified exactly by verifying~\eqref{eq:third-schedule} at the four vertices of $\mathcal{R}$.

Operating-cost perturbations can thus shift the switching threshold of an active $(A,B)$ competition regime via~\eqref{eq:threshold-shift}, whereas network changes can admit new feasible schedules that supersede the $(A,B)$ regime via~\eqref{eq:third-schedule}. Under any market condition $S$ (specifying cost parameters and transmission limits), the strategic generator's additional actual profit at offer $\delta$ is evaluated relative to that condition's own truthful-offer baseline:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Pi_g^S(\delta) = \Pi_g^S(\delta) - \Pi_g^S(0),\label{eq:condition-profit}
\end{equation}
combining the condition-specific CHP price response with complete LOC settlement.

The principal economic case is a modified IEEE 30-bus system with six generators. A modified IEEE 118-bus system with 54 generators provides a larger repeated-pricing workload. Both systems use a 24-hour horizon, three incremental cost segments, and PTDF transmission constraints. All units are initially offline at zero output, with minimum down times satisfied; physical clearing, CHP, and self-scheduling share these inherited conditions.

Offers are displayed as percentages of a fixed reference incremental slope: the normalized adder $\delta/\sigma_g^{\rm ref}$ ranges from 0 to 50\%. The interval representation supplies the continuous assessment with a finite set of settlement evaluations. \section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A continuous strategic-offer assessment can be constructed by combining selected CHP prices, executable UC schedules, and complete reported LOC settlement. Common optimality information supports the price and physical intervals, and their intersection reduces actual-profit evaluation to finite endpoint/physical-schedule candidates. The same price map provides computational reuse across repeated queries. This connects parametric pricing directly to the task of evaluating a producer's profitable offers over a declared range.

The principal case reveals how price-related gains accumulate before costly generation substitution changes the profit path. G5 can replace G1 earlier in the day, but the additional online expense delays the point at which that replacement becomes economic. Changing this expense moves the supported substitution threshold. Relaxing transmission instead admits a different competing schedule. These findings connect the operating choices available to the system with actual producer gain under CHP. Evaluating both price responses and competing complete schedules provides a consistent basis for examining how a specified offer opportunity changes with market conditions.

\section{Global Competition and Exclusion by a Feasible Schedule}\label{app:competition}
In network condition $S$, a fixed schedule has offered cost $\ell_j(\delta,\epsilon)=C_j+\epsilon\chi_j+\delta Q_j$. For feasible A and B, define their gap to the globally optimal UC value as
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_{AB}^{S}(\delta,\epsilon)=\min_{j\in\{A,B\}}\ell_j(\delta,\epsilon)-v_S^{\UC}(\delta,\epsilon).\label{eq:competition-gap}
\end{equation}
A positive gap excludes the pair at that condition. A supported A/B switching threshold requires that their cost lines attain the global value at the crossing and remain optimal on the corresponding adjacent sides. The global bounds and common-schedule tests in Section~\ref{sec:method} provide these checks.

A feasible schedule C can exclude both alternatives over a parameter rectangle $\mathcal R=[a,b]\times[\epsilon_-,\epsilon_+]$. It suffices that
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \ell_j(\delta,\epsilon)-\ell_C(\delta,\epsilon)>0,\qquad j\in\{A,B\},\\
 (\delta,\epsilon)\in\operatorname{corners}(\mathcal R).
 \end{gathered}\label{eq:competitor-exclusion}
\end{equation}
Each fixed-schedule difference is affine in the two parameters, and its minimum on the rectangle occurs at a corner. Positivity at every corner thus excludes A and B throughout the rectangle. C's feasibility and its lower offered cost suffice for this comparison. In the relaxed-network experiment, its minimum advantage is $1{,}635.46$ over $[0,20.991]\times[0,5]$. Global UC additionally supports C at the four evaluated positive-offer conditions. These are distinct statements: rectangle-wide exclusion of A/B and pointwise support for C.

\section{Settlement Comparisons with Transmission Constraints}\label{app:network-settlement}
For a network condition $S$, write $\Delta_\delta\lambda^S=\lambda^S(\delta)-\lambda^S(0)$. Relaxed ($r$) and original ($o$) networks can be compared relative to their respective truthful baselines through
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_{n,t}^{\lambda}(\delta)&=\Delta_\delta\lambda_{n,t}^{r}-\Delta_\delta\lambda_{n,t}^{o},\label{eq:network-response}\\
 \mathcal I_g^{\Pi}(\delta)&=\Delta\Pi_g^{r}-\Delta\Pi_g^{o}\notag\\
 &= (\Delta R_g^{r}-\Delta R_g^{o})+(\Delta U_g^{r}-\Delta U_g^{o})\notag\\
 &\quad -(\Delta C_g^{r}-\Delta C_g^{o}).\label{eq:network-profit-response}
\end{align}
These are deterministic response contrasts with complete settlement accounting. For the first fixed offer 13.2030804436 USD/MWh and $\epsilon=0$, original- and relaxed-network baseline profits are $37{,}289.31$ and $31{,}911.46$, respectively. Their additional profits are $7{,}810.84$ and $8{,}390.69$. The change in additional profit is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_{G1}^{\Pi}=-2127.85+59.92+2647.77=579.84\ \mathrm{USD},\label{eq:network-response-result}
\end{equation}
where the terms are revenue, uplift, and negative actual-cost response differences, respectively. The supplementary factorial accounts apply the same baseline convention to both online-cost conditions and both fixed offers.

The network settlement check uses the transmission adjustment
\begin{equation}
 \Delta^{\mathrm{TX}}=\sum_{\ell,t}
 [\mu_{\ell,t}^+(F_\ell-f_{\ell,t}^{\UC})
 +\mu_{\ell,t}^-(F_\ell+f_{\ell,t}^{\UC})].\label{eq:tx}
\end{equation}
With $D^{\lambda}=\sum_{n,t}\lambda_{n,t}^{\CHP}D_{n,t}$, strong duality and unit self-scheduling yield
\begin{equation}
 v^{\CHP}=D^{\lambda}-\sum_{\ell,t}(\mu_{\ell,t}^++\mu_{\ell,t}^-)F_\ell-\sum_i\widehat\Pi_i^{\SSched}.\label{eq:dual-bridge}
\end{equation}
Nodal pricing and physical balance also give $\sum_iR_i-D^{\lambda}=-\sum_{\ell,t}(\mu_{\ell,t}^+-\mu_{\ell,t}^-)f_{\ell,t}^{\UC}$. Substituting into~\eqref{eq:uplift} gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i U_i+\Delta^{\mathrm{TX}}=v^{\UC}(\delta)-v^{\CHP}(\delta).\label{eq:network-settlement}
\end{equation}
Both objective values use reported costs. The nonnegative adjustment evaluates pricing multipliers against physical scheduled flows. In the principal case it falls from $548.26$ at truthful bidding to $334.55$ at the highest profit candidate. Together with total uplift, it reconciles the reported UC--CHP cost gap. Detailed nodal prices, line multipliers, physical flows, and all 16 physical switching-cost accounts are retained as source data.
