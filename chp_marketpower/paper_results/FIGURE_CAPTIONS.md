# Figure captions and table notes

**Fig. 1. Repeated CHP pricing under fixed market conditions.** Measured cumulative times for (a) IEEE30-G1, (b) IEEE30-G2 and (c) IEEE118-G11. The 30-bus streams each contain 1,000 queries and the 118-bus stream contains 200, in their original shuffled order. Persistent reoptimization includes model setup, objective updates, optimization and raw-price extraction. Parametric costs include archived construction, postprocessing, file loading and measured online lookup. Open markers identify the first observed amortized integer query. No crossing is extrapolated for the 118-bus case. These are single measured streams, not repeated samples; system UC and uplift evaluation are excluded.

**Table I. Parametric CHP representation and computational performance.** Source data retain construction dimensions, value/price intervals, physical-region counts and each timing component. Not-assessed entries do not denote zero complexity or zero profit. G1 has 99 joint intervals and 116 offer/physical-plan candidates at 100 distinct offers under the declared rules.

**Fig. 2. Energy and congestion components of strategic-offer price responses.** (a) G1-node CHP in the physical start hour 1, a tied largest positive price-effect hour 2, and baseline peak-demand hour 19. The offer changes every hour. (b) System energy, congestion and total CHP components at the G5 node in hour 19. (c) Signed hourly contributions Σ p^SS dλ to G1's price-related profit effect from truthful bidding to the best candidate. Original PTDF reference bus 1 is retained; G1 is located at this reference, so its explicit congestion-price component is zero by convention. This is not evidence that transmission constraints have no influence on G1. Hour 2 is selected descriptively from the calculated contributions; no significance test is implied. Full-dual interpolation and nodal-price continuity were checked, and component allocations are not independent causal effects.

**Fig. 3. Strategic profits and uplift payments over the complete offer range.** Additional G1 actual profit and full reported lost-opportunity-cost uplift payment share the same absolute offer-adder axis. Each continuous segment uses a certified common physical schedule and optimal self-schedule; no line interpolates across a physical commitment change. Dotted vertical indicators and open right-side markers show jumps/alternative adjacent boundary limits, not intermediate attainable payoffs. The star is the declared higher-profit physical boundary candidate at δ=13.2040804436 USD/MWh. Baseline profit is USD37,289.31 and the additional profit is USD7,811.56. Uplift falls from USD594.61 to approximately USD16.26 at this candidate. Attainment depends on the stated price and boundary execution rules; values are not bounds over all legal CHP price selections.

**Table II. Changes in generator revenues, uplift, actual costs and profits.** Best-offer minus truthful-bid amounts use the same declared higher-profit boundary schedule for all units. Every row satisfies ΔΠ=ΔR+ΔU−ΔC. Other units do not change their own offers. Negative cost changes mean lower actual operating cost, and profit changes need not be zero-sum. Absolute amounts and reference-dependent energy/congestion revenue components are available in the source table.

**Fig. 4. Generation substitution, offered system-cost competition and a predicted threshold.** (a) Changes from schedule A to B in the first six hours: G5 starts five hours earlier and replaces G1/G3 generation. (b) Actual offered system-cost lines for A/B, with their same offer-dependent pair mean subtracted solely for visual separation; this common subtraction preserves the crossing. (c) The analytical threshold prediction for the predefined G5 online-cost changes and independent global-UC support. The +5 case is a predefined within-system comparison, not an independent-system replicate or a fitted regression. A separate +50 USD/start actual startup-cost control leaves the threshold unchanged because both plans start G5 once. It is not a strategic startup-offer experiment. Prediction validity requires that the same candidates remain globally optimal around the crossing, as checked against global UC bounds.
