**Fig. 3. Operating costs and network feasibility change competition differently.** Source `figures/fig2_competition_conditions`. Panel a plots the analytically predicted original-network A/B crossing at G5 online-cost changes of zero and +5 USD/hour, with global UC support at both conditions. Panel b plots offered-cost differences A−C and B−C over δ∈[0,20.991]. Solid and dashed curves are the zero and +5 cost conditions. Fixed-schedule differences are affine in both parameters; the four corner comparisons give a minimum positive advantage of 1,635.464038 USD for C over A/B across the rectangle. C is feasible in the relaxed network. Its global optimality at the four evaluated positive-offer conditions is checked separately. The figure displays deterministic schedule-cost relations, with no statistical replicates or uncertainty bands.

**Fig. 4. Computational reuse over measured CHP-query workloads.** Source `revision_20261020/figures/fig1_cumulative_pricing_time`. The 30-G1 and 30-G2 streams each contain 1,000 queries. The 118-G11 Q500 and Q200 streams are independent, measured in their archived shuffled order. Parametric totals include construction, postprocessing, loading and online lookup; direct totals include persistent setup, objective updates, optimization and raw-price extraction. Physical UC and self-scheduling are separate computations. The source preserves all four streams; no timing curve is extrapolated or stitched from separate streams.
